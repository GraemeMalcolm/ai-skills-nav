---
title: Understand Microsoft Copilot Chat
---

Copilot Chat's usefulness depends on where it's grounding its responses—your organization's data, the public web, or both. Understanding this distinction is the key to directing Copilot to the right sources and getting responses you can act on.

For example, if you ask What decisions were made in last week's project meetings?, you want Copilot using your work data. If you ask What are current industry trends in retail?, you want it using the web.

Depending on your license and interface, you may see this reflected differently:

- Premium users may experience this as Work IQ, where Copilot can reason across your work data and the web
- Other users may see options to switch between work and web grounding

In either case, the goal is the same: make sure Copilot is using the right data source for your task.

Copilot also keeps context within a conversation. Each message builds on what came before, so you can refine or expand your request without repeating details. When you start a new chat, that context resets.

## How Copilot understands your request

Copilot doesn't just match keywords. It understands the meaning behind your request, so you can ask questions naturally.

For example, a prompt about "financial risks" might surface content related to "budget concerns" because Copilot recognizes the relationship between those ideas.

When Copilot has access to your work data, it can apply this same understanding across your emails, files, meetings, and chats. This helps it return responses that reflect your actual work context, not just a list of loosely related matches.

This is enabled by Work IQ, which allows Copilot to connect information across your day-to-day work. Instead of treating each file or message separately, it can recognize how your work fits together—who you collaborate with, what projects you're involved in, and what's most relevant to your request right now. It also respects your permissions. Copilot only returns content you already have access to, and all responses follow your organization's security and compliance policies.

## Choose how Copilot responds

Copilot Chat includes a model selector that controls how it reasons through your prompt. The default setting is Auto, which routes your request to the most appropriate model based on complexity.

You can also set it manually:

- Quick response - Best for routine or straightforward questions. Copilot prioritizes speed and returns concise answers.
- Think deeper - Best for complex, open-ended, or multi-step questions. Copilot takes more time to plan, gather context, and verify its reasoning before responding.

Use Think deeper when the quality and completeness of the answer matter more than speed.

## Access Copilot Chat

You can open Copilot Chat from several places, but two are the most common:

- Outlook side pane Select the Copilot icon on the right side of your inbox to open chat alongside your email.

- Microsoft Copilot app Go to Open Microsoft Copilot and sign in with your work account. This is the main hub for chat, agents, files, and search.

If you don't see Copilot Chat in your apps, your organization's admin settings may control where it appears. You can always access it directly from the Copilot app in your browser.

> Note: The Copilot side pane in Outlook provides a full chat experience. This is separate from Copilot features built into individual emails or meetings.

## Find information fast with Copilot Search

Copilot Chat is designed for creating, summarizing, and working through tasks. When you need to quickly find something specific, Copilot Search is often faster.

Copilot Search is available as a Search tab in the Microsoft Copilot app. It lets you search across your Microsoft 365 data and connected systems from a single place.
