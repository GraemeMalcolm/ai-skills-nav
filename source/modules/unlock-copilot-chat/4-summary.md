title: Summary
---

In this module, you explored how Microsoft Copilot Chat works and how to move beyond basic prompting to get more meaningful results. You saw that Copilot's effectiveness depends on what information it can access.

When grounded in your work data, Copilot can pull from your emails, files, meetings, and chats to return results based on your actual work context. On top of that, Work IQ and semantic indexing help Copilot understand the meaning behind your prompts, not just the words, so it can surface the most relevant content even when there's no exact match.

When Copilot brings in information from the web, it can use public sources to support research, comparisons, and external analysis. If you have a premium license, you can combine both, allowing Copilot to draw from your organizational data and up-to-date web content in a single prompt.

You also learned how to direct Copilot toward the right sources and get better output. The model selector lets you choose between Quick response for routine tasks and Think deeper for complex, multi-step reasoning. Prompt Coach helps you diagnose weak prompts and rebuild them with the context, scope, and structure Copilot needs. You were also introduced to the Researcher agent, which handles complex, multi-step research by synthesizing findings from multiple sources—available to users with a premium license. Using Copilot Chat, you can bring your own content into any conversation by uploading a file, referencing a SharePoint or OneDrive file with /, or relying on open-content awareness in Outlook and Teams. When a response is worth keeping, Copilot Pages turns it into a persistent, editable canvas you can share and build on over time.

You also explored Copilot Search, the dedicated Search tab in the Microsoft Copilot app. While Chat is designed for generating content and completing tasks, Copilot Search is designed to quickly find specific files, emails, people, and resources across your data. When a search result needs deeper exploration, you can continue the conversation directly in Chat without switching context.

Finally, you saw how to personalize the Microsoft Copilot app in two ways: by customizing copilot with instructions that tell Copilot standing information about your role, communication preferences, and response format so you don't have to repeat it in every prompt; and by pinning the apps and agents you use most often to your left navigation pane so your most important tools are always one click away.

You applied these skills in a hands-on exercise using the Microsoft Copilot app, where you prepared for an interview by researching a company and role, generating and drafting answers to anticipated questions, saving your preparation as a Copilot Page, and drafting a follow-up note in Outlook.
